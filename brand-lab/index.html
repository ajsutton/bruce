A home app should have
a point of view.

Four working identities built from the house’s angular roof, blue-hour light, warm interiors, water and subtropical green—without depicting the property.

These are design territories, not finished trademark clearances. Judge the feeling first; we can sharpen or rename the strongest system.

Bruce.

The house is
sorted.

Bruce is down-to-earth, capable and doing rather well for himself. He likes the good stuff but never makes a production of it. No sci-fi. No dashboard theatre. Just a comfortable house that behaves itself.

Lead direction

Personable in voice, restrained in the interface, memorable everywhere else.

B

The character

Good taste.
No carry-on.

Bruce knows the pool temperature, whether the garage is shut and which lights the kids left on. He doesn’t announce that he’s “leveraging intelligent automation.” He just sorts it.

01Down to earth

Plain language, direct answers and no needless ceremony.

02Quietly successful

Beautiful materials and confident restraint, never conspicuous luxury.

03Properly helpful

Bruce reports what matters, acts quickly and admits when something failed.

A second naming axis

Someone you ask.
Not something you operate.

A human name makes the app feel less like infrastructure and gives future Siri actions an easy spoken shape. The danger is drifting into fake-assistant theatre, so the personality should stay concise, capable and never chatty for its own sake.

THE TEST SENTENCE

“Hey Siri, ask Marnie to cool the house.”

Non-negotiables in every direction

Native first.
Brand in the seams.

Standard navigation, typography, materials and controls on every Apple platform. Identity lives in iconography, voice, selection, rhythm and a few memorable moments—not a custom design system pasted over SwiftUI.
